improvements for the CV model : 
Here’s where I’d focus.

Mine your mistakes aggressively

This is the highest ROI step.

After training:

run inference on all training data
collect:
false positives
false negatives
low confidence samples (0.4 to 0.6 probability)

Then inspect them manually.

Why:
Your model is telling you where the boundary is unclear.

Usually you’ll find:

mislabeled images
weird lighting
borderline defects
crop failures
duplicate-looking samples

Even 1 to 3 percent label noise hurts a lot with 36k.

In industrial datasets, cleaning 500 bad labels often beats adding 10k images.

Hard negative mining

You already have sampler balancing, but not hard mining.

Do this:

after epoch 5 or after first training
identify negatives predicted as faulty with high confidence

Oversample them in next round.

Pseudo-flow:

round 1:
normal training

collect:
hard_negatives = false positives

round 2:
train with:
train_set + 2x hard_negatives

This sharpens decision boundaries.

For defect detection, this is often a major jump.

Higher resolution

You currently resize to 260 because of EfficientNet-B2/B0.

For subtle rim defects, tiny scratches or dents matter.

Try:

EfficientNet-B3 or B4
300 to 380 px

Example:

B3 = 300
B4 = 380

Tradeoff:
slower training, often better defect sensitivity.

Since you have 36k, model capacity is less risky.

Two-view training

This is often huge for industrial inspection.

Instead of one crop:
train on:

tight rim crop
slightly wider crop

Reason:
Some defects need local texture.
Others need context.

Options:
A.
Train two separate models.

model_local
model_global

Average logits.

Or B.
Concatenate both as channels.

Ensembling often adds 1 to 3 percent.

Replace BCE with focal loss

Since defects are rare and likely heterogeneous:

BCE is fine, but focal loss often better.

Why:
focuses on hard examples.

Typical:

gamma=2.0
alpha=0.25

This often reduces easy negative domination.

Especially useful when:

many clean images
few tricky defects
Use test-time augmentation

Cheap improvement.

At inference:

average predictions over:

original
horizontal flip
slight rotation
brightness shift

Example:
pred = mean([
model(img),
model(hflip(img)),
model(rot(img)),
])

Often small but reliable gain.

Better augmentations for industrial images

Your current augmentations are decent, but a bit generic.

Less useful:

RandomPerspective
strong rotations, depending on domain

More useful:

CLAHE
slight blur
noise injection
brightness/contrast shifts
small affine transforms

Industrial defects are texture-sensitive.

Albumentations usually beats torchvision here.

Strong candidates:

CLAHE
RandomBrightnessContrast
MotionBlur
GaussNoise
Ensemble models

If leaderboard score matters, ensembles are the boring truth.

Train:

EfficientNet-B0
EfficientNet-B2
ResNet50 or ConvNeXt tiny

Average logits.

Why:
Different architectures make different mistakes.

Ensemble often beats single fancy model.

Separate threshold by business goal

Do not optimize only F1.

Sometimes leaderboard or real metric rewards something else.

For example:

maximize recall under FP constraint
maximize precision for faulty class

Try threshold sweeps on:

F1
recall
precision
custom metric

Sometimes score gain is pure thresholding.

Pseudo-labeling if unlabeled data exists

If you have extra unlabeled images:

infer with high confidence
keep predictions >0.98 or <0.02
retrain

This is free extra data.

Very effective when data distribution matches.
